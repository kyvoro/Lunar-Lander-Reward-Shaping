# Experiment plan

Status: the random baseline, one 1,000,000-step PPO baseline, and 100,000-step pilots for both reward variants have been executed. The equal-budget multi-seed comparison remains planned. Planned methods below are not results.

## Random baseline result

Ten random-action episodes were run with environment and action-space seeds 0–9. Mean original episode return was -173.23; individual returns ranged from -279.90 to -81.49. All ten ended with `terminated=True` and `truncated=False`. These flags do not distinguish a crash from a safe resting landing, so the return values alone do not establish a landing success rate. Per-episode values and lengths are in `results/random_baseline.csv`.

## Initial PPO run

PPO with `MlpPolicy` was trained for 100,000 requested steps using seed 0 and the original Gymnasium reward. PPO completed its rollout and trained for 100,352 steps. The saved model was evaluated deterministically on seeds 100–109 in an unmodified environment.

Mean original episode return was -59.81, with a range from -84.65 to -43.90. This is less negative than the random baseline mean, but the comparison is preliminary and uses only ten evaluation episodes. All ten PPO episodes reached the 1,000-step time limit (`terminated=False`, `truncated=True`); none completed before the limit. Therefore this run has not demonstrated a landing. The evaluation records are in `results/ppo_baseline_eval.csv`; the 100,000-step model is `models/ppo_baseline_100k_seed_0.zip`.

An episode video can be recorded with `record_baseline.py` for qualitative inspection. A time-limit outcome alone does not establish whether the policy hovered, drifted, or made contact without settling; inspect the video before describing the behavior.

## One-million-step baseline, training seed 0

The baseline script was then run for 1,000,000 requested steps. PPO completed its rollout at 1,001,472 steps. The model was evaluated deterministically on the same separate seeds 100–109, using the original reward and a 1,000-step episode limit.

Mean original return was 214.19, ranging from 119.78 to 259.49. Seven of ten episodes scored at least 200, which Gymnasium defines as the solution threshold for an episode. Nine episodes terminated before the time limit; one was truncated at 1,000 steps. Treat the score threshold as the documented solution measure, not as a separately observed physical-landing counter. Per-episode returns and flags are in `results/ppo_baseline_eval.csv`.

The deterministic seed-100 recording from this model is generated by `record_baseline.py` in `videos/ppo_baseline_1m/`. The 100,000-step recording is kept separately in `videos/ppo_baseline/`.

## Equal-budget pilot comparison

Three seed-0 policies were trained for 100,000 requested steps (100,352 actual steps each) and evaluated deterministically on the same environment seeds 100–109 with the reward wrapper disabled. These are development pilots, not the planned final comparison.

| Training reward | Mean original evaluation reward | Episodes at 200+ | Time-limit episodes |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original reward | -59.81 | 0/10 | 10/10 |
| Original + hover bonus | -71.94 | 0/10 | 10/10 |
| Original - time penalty | -97.34 | 0/10 | 8/10 |

During hover-bonus training, the monitor recorded 332 completed episodes with mean original return -103.95, mean added bonus +156.23, and mean combined return +52.28. Fifty-seven training episodes reached the 1,000-step limit. This gap shows that the bonus can raise the training score while the original task score remains poor. The deterministic seed-100 video also ends with the lander airborne above the pad after 1,000 steps. Together these pilot observations are consistent with the intended reward-hacking failure mode, but they do not establish how often it will occur under the final training budget or other training seeds.

During time-penalty training, 363 episodes had mean original return -66.83 and mean added penalty -13.75, for a combined mean of -80.57; 24 training episodes reached the time limit. Its ten-episode original-reward evaluation had no score at or above 200. This short run does not show that the correction improves performance; it needs the same longer budget and multiple training seeds before conclusions.

Evaluation CSVs are `results/ppo_baseline_100k_pilot.csv`, `results/hover_bonus_100k_eval.csv`, and `results/time_penalty_100k_eval.csv`. Per-episode training reward components are stored in the `.monitor.csv` files. The [hover-bonus seed-100 clip](../videos/hover_bonus_100k/ppo_hover_bonus_seed_0_100000-seed-100-episode-0.mp4) shows the lander still airborne at the end of that pilot episode.

## Landing-zone follow-up, training seed 0

The learner later clarified that a successful touchdown must fit fully inside the marked pad. The one-million-step PPO landing-zone model completed 1,001,472 steps with training seed 0 and was evaluated on development seeds 100–109. Eight of ten episodes placed both complete leg fixtures inside the pad. Nine episodes had a stable resting landing, one ended without a stable resting landing, and none crashed. All ten scored at least 200 and terminated before the time limit, showing why the score threshold is not a substitute for the physical zone metric. Mean original reward was 280.32. This is one model and ten evaluation seeds; it does not establish a reliable success rate across training seeds.

Per-episode state, crash, stable-landing, and pad-success fields are saved in `results/landing_zone_seed_0_eval.csv`. The training reward components are in `results/training_landing_zone_seed_0_1000000.monitor.csv`. The model is `models/ppo_landing_zone_seed_0_1000000.zip`.

## Variants

| Variant | Training reward | Hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Original environment reward | PPO learns to land. |
| Hover bonus | Original reward plus 1 per qualifying step | Remaining airborne may become preferable to landing. |
| Time-penalty correction | Original reward minus 0.05 per step; no hover bonus | Waiting becomes less attractive. |
| Landing-zone shaping | Original reward plus pad-center progress and terminal zone feedback | The lander is more likely to settle with both legs inside the marked pad. |

The landing-zone variant is a follow-up objective added after visual inspection showed that the seed-100 baseline could rest with one leg beyond the pad edge. It adds `20 × (previous horizontal distance to pad center − current distance)` each step, `+100` for a stable landing with both complete Box2D leg fixtures inside the pad, and `−50` for a stable landing outside it. Crashes receive no additional zone penalty, so an off-pad resting landing remains preferable to crashing. The experiment must report physical zone success independently of original return and the 200-point threshold.

The implemented hover condition is `abs(angle) < 0.2`, Euclidean speed below `0.2` in the observation's scaled units, height above `0.1` in observation units, and neither leg in contact with the ground. Height prevents rewarding an agent merely resting just above the surface. These constants are fixed in `reward_wrappers.py` for the pilot; freeze them before the main comparison.

The correction changes two aspects relative to the hover variant: it removes the hover bonus and applies a small cost on every step. Compare each variant with the original-reward baseline as a common reference. Hovering and performance improvements are hypotheses, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Protocol

- First verify the pipeline with short runs.
- Use PPO with `MlpPolicy`, the same training settings, and the same episode limit for all variants.
- Train each variant from scratch with seeds 0, 1, and 2.
- Start with 1,000,000 environment steps per run. Record actual steps, because PPO may finish its current rollout beyond the requested budget. Any budget increase applies equally to every variant.
- Use separate development evaluation seeds. Freeze reward rules and training settings before the final evaluation.
- Evaluate every final model deterministically on 100 episodes using seeds 10000 through 10099. Do not use those episodes to tune the project.
- Use an evaluation environment without reward modifications. Preserve the original episode limit.
- Record original reward and added reward separately during training.

## Reporting

Report original episode return (mean and standard deviation), the fraction of episodes at or above Gymnasium's 200-point solution threshold, stable landing count, landing-zone success count, episode length, and time-limit truncations. Report each training seed separately as well as aggregate results. Do not label the fraction of returns above 200 as an independently measured physical landing rate.

The performance target for the baseline and correction is mean original return of at least 200. Missing that target is a reportable result. A successful resting landing according to the environment does not independently prove a landing between the flags.

Videos illustrate behaviour; quantitative evaluation establishes how frequent it is. A hover failure that does not emerge must not be described as observed reward hacking.

## Checks before full training

- Verify reward calculations with known inputs.
- Verify wrapper reset behaviour and preservation of observations and episode-ending signals.
- Verify that a resting landing with any leg outside the pad is not counted as a zone success.
- Verify a short train/save/load/evaluate cycle.
- Verify original-reward evaluation independently of reward additions.

Reproducible scripts, pinned dependencies, per-run metadata and CSV results, comparison plots, labelled videos, and an English README explaining methods, findings, limitations, and sources.
